ASSET QUERY RULES (FOR BROLL_CAPTION ONLY)
==================================================

For `broll_caption`, you MUST explicitly generate a concrete `asset_query`.
It is REQUIRED and must NEVER be null for `broll_caption`.

CRITICAL ARCHITECTURAL CONTEXT:
- `asset_query` is NOT displayed to the viewer.
- It is NOT a caption, title, or summary.
- It exists ONLY to search the stock-media provider (Pexels / Pixabay).
- `visual_goal` describes what the viewer should understand.
- `asset_query` describes what should physically appear on camera.
- NEVER mix these two concepts.

MANDATORY RULES:

A. Concrete visual language grounded in the scene's actual domain:
The queries MUST contain concrete visual nouns and visible actions matching what is actually happening in the scene.
For personal finance, salary, career, investing, or budgeting topics:
Prefer:
- person
- young professional
- employee
- office
- desk
- laptop
- bank account on phone
- currency notes
- investment portfolio
- stock market charts
- bills
- calculator
- bank branch
- signing contract

NEVER default to cars, mechanics, tires, or dealerships unless the video topic or scene is explicitly about automobiles!

B. Search-engine style & multi-query candidates:
Target approximately 2–6 words per query.
Provide 2–3 distinct candidate queries in `asset_queries` ordered by preference, with the best candidate in `asset_query`.
Natural, concise search phrases that match real footage in stock libraries.
Examples:
- Personal finance / salary: ["person checking bank account on phone", "person calculating monthly expenses", "young professional in modern office"]
- Investments / Compounding: ["person reviewing investments", "stock market charts on laptop", "person analyzing financial report"]
- Budgeting / Debt / Expenses: ["person reviewing household bills", "person calculating expenses", "calculator with monthly budget"]
- Career / Office: ["young professional working in modern office", "business colleagues talking in office", "person typing on laptop desk"]
- Banking / Paperwork: ["person reviewing financial documents", "signing bank contract", "person inside modern bank"]
- Automotive (ONLY if scene is about cars): ["car dealership showroom", "driver filling car fuel", "car driving on highway"]

C. Search intent, not explanation:
Do NOT write what the viewer should understand or realize.
BAD: "Viewer understands the bank's risk perspective"
GOOD: "person reviewing loan application documents"
BAD: "Viewer recognizes lifestyle inflation"
GOOD: "person shopping in luxury store"
BAD: "Viewer realizes hidden ongoing expenses"
GOOD: "person stressed reviewing monthly bills"

D. No sentence fragments or full sentences:
Never return:
- narration excerpts
- full sentences
- explanatory clauses
- "viewer..." phrases
- thesis statements
- educational definitions

E. No invisible concepts unless they have a concrete visual representation:
For abstract concepts such as:
- "opportunity cost" → "person comparing investments on laptop"
- "wealth erosion" → "person calculating monthly bills"
- "financial discipline" → "person reviewing savings account on phone"
Do NOT search the abstract phrase directly unless stock providers actually support that term usefully.
Instead select a concrete visual representation grounded in the narration.
Do NOT invent a visual situation that contradicts the narration.

F. Preserve domain specificity:
Use the actual subject.
For a car-finance video:
BAD: "business meeting"
GOOD: "car dealership finance office"
BAD: "financial planning"
GOOD: "person reviewing car expenses"

G. Numbers:
Do not depend on exact numbers being searchable.
For "₹5,000 tires rising to ₹20,000", prefer: "mechanic replacing car tire" (not "5000 20000 tire").
For "₹25,000 EMI", prefer: "car loan payment paperwork" (not "25000 rupees EMI").
Stock search should generally use the underlying physical scene.

FOR ALL OTHER COMPOSITIONS (NON-B-ROLL):
`asset_query` MUST be null.
Do NOT request an asset for infographic compositions.

METRIC — metric_hero

Use for one dominant metric or value.

--------------------------------------------------
CALCULATION — calculation_story
--------------------------------------------------

Use when the viewer needs to understand a mathematical transformation or financial calculation:

input
→ operation (optional, defaults to '→' or math symbol)
→ modifier/rate (optional)
→ result

Required fields:

- `input_label`
- `input_value`
- `result_label`
- `result_value`

Optional fields:

- `operation_label` (e.g. '×', '÷', '−', '+', '→'. Defaults to '→' or math symbol)
- `rate_label` (e.g. '4% withdrawal rate', '12% return', '20 years'. Can be omitted for direct transformations)
- `operation_type` ('multiplication' | 'addition' | 'subtraction' | 'growth' | 'neutral')
- `secondary_label` (e.g. 'Total Invested')
- `secondary_value` (e.g. '₹18 Lakh')
- `timeframe` (e.g. 'over 15 years')
- `note` (e.g. 'Compounded at 12%')
- `variant` ('growth' | 'multiplication' | 'addition' | 'subtraction' | 'neutral')

Use the exact factual inputs/results from the VisualIntent.

You may refine:

- `input_label`
- `result_label`
- `operation_label`
- `rate_label`
- `note`
- `variant`
- `operation_type`

Do not invent a calculation.

Do not insert an arbitrary operator just because it looks visually attractive.

The operation must reflect the relationship actually expressed in the narration.

--------------------------------------------------

cause

`steps`

--------------------------------------------------
DIVERGENCE — trajectory_divergence
--------------------------------------------------

Use when two financial paths or strategies evolve in opposite directions from a common starting point over time, and the central viewer insight is the growing gap between them.

NOT for static side-by-side comparisons at one point in time — use `comparison_split` for those.

Fields:

- `time_horizon`: Duration over which the divergence unfolds (required — e.g. '10 Years', '7 Years')
- `baseline_label`: What both paths share at the start (e.g. '₹30,000 Monthly Commitment')
- `path_a`: Favorable/growing path:
  - `label`: Path name (e.g. 'Investor (Equity SIP)')
  - `end_value`: Terminal value if stated in narration (e.g. '₹38 Lakh'). NEVER fabricate.
  - `rate`: Return rate if explicitly stated (e.g. '12% CAGR'). NEVER fabricate.
  - `direction`: 'up' | 'down' | 'neutral'
  - `tone`: 'positive' | 'negative' | 'neutral'
- `path_b`: Unfavorable/declining path (same fields as path_a)
- `divergence_gap`: Explicit gap value if stated in narration (e.g. '₹32 Lakh Wealth Gap'). NEVER fabricate.
- `header_label`: Category eyebrow, e.g. 'WEALTH ACCUMULATION DIVERGENCE'
- `variant`: 'divergence' | 'wealth_gap' | 'cost_opportunity' | 'standard'

Do not invent path end values, rates, or the gap amount.

Do not use for a comparison that is purely static (no time evolution, no growing gap).

--------------------------------------------------
WATERFALL — cash_flow_waterfall
--------------------------------------------------

Use when a starting total resource (salary, corpus, revenue) is sequentially depleted by labeled deductions, and the residual after all deductions is the key viewer insight.

NOT for a single arithmetic operation — use `calculation_story` for those.
NOT for sequential procedural steps — use `process_flow` for those.

Required:

- `starting_label`: Label for the initial resource (e.g. 'Gross Monthly Salary', 'Starting Corpus')
- `starting_value`: The initial total amount
- `steps`: Ordered list of deductions (2–6 steps):
  - `label`: Name of the deduction (e.g. 'Taxes', 'EMI Obligations')
  - `value`: Signed string (e.g. '-₹1,50,000'). Negative values = deductions.
  - `direction`: 'subtract' | 'add'
  - `subtext`: Optional brief clarifying note (e.g. 'Direct Tax Code'). Never fabricate.

Optional:

- `final_value`: Remaining balance after all deductions. Only populate if derivable from explicit narration values or stated directly. NEVER fabricate.
- `final_label`: Label for the remainder (e.g. 'Investable Surplus', 'Net Cash')
- `header_label`: Category eyebrow, e.g. 'MONTHLY CASH FLOW'
- `variant`: 'standard' | 'detailed' | 'compact'

Steps must follow narration order. Do not invent intermediate balances or additional deduction categories.

--------------------------------------------------
GROWTH — growth_trajectory
--------------------------------------------------

Use for a single quantity, asset, corpus, or financial state growing upward over time (linear savings, compounding snowball, accelerating returns, milestone progression).

Fields:

- `header_label`: Category title, e.g. 'WEALTH ACCUMULATION', 'CORPUS GROWTH', 'COMPOUNDING TRAJECTORY'
- `start_value`: Starting quantity if mentioned (e.g. '₹0', '₹50,000/mo', '₹10 Lakh')
- `start_label`: Starting point label (e.g. 'Initial Savings', 'Base Corpus', 'Monthly Contribution')
- `end_value`: Eventual / target quantity if mentioned (e.g. '₹10 Lakh', '₹1 Crore', '₹38 Lakh')
- `end_label`: Target label (e.g. 'Target Corpus', 'Accumulated Wealth', 'Eventual Corpus')
- `time_horizon`: Optional duration (e.g. '7 years', '10 years', 'over time')
- `growth_rate`: Optional explicit rate if mentioned in narration (e.g. '12% Annual Return', '10% CAGR'). NEVER fabricate.
- `growth_type`: 'linear' | 'accelerating' | 'compound' | 'unspecified'
- `milestone_value`: Optional intermediate milestone (e.g. '₹10 Lakh', 'First ₹10 Lakh')
- `milestone_label`: Optional milestone label (e.g. 'Inflection Point', 'Tipping Point', 'Snowball Begins')
- `annotation`: Optional key insight or callout note
- `variant`: 'linear_accumulation' | 'accelerating_growth' | 'compounding_snowball' | 'milestone_progression' | 'standard'

Do not invent intermediate trajectory points.

Do not invent annual returns or growth rates if not stated.

==================================================
FINAL QUALITY CONTROL
==================================================

Before returning the JSON, silently check:

### 1. Catalog compliance

Is the composition ID actually registered?

### 2. Relationship compliance

Does the selected composition support this exact relationship type?

### 3. Trend safety

If relationship type is `trend`, is the trend explicitly declining?

If not, return `no_suitable_composition`.

### 4. Schema compliance

Does `composition_data` contain every required catalog field?

Are all field names exact?

Are there any invented fields?

### 5. Factual integrity

Are every number, value, entity, relationship, ranking, and outcome consistent with the supplied VisualIntent?

### 6. No fabrication

Did you introduce anything that was not supported upstream?

### 7. Trigger integrity

Did you copy `intent.trigger_word` exactly?

### 8. Asset integrity

Is asset requirement consistent with the selected composition?

For `broll_caption`:
- Is `asset_query` present, non-null, and a concrete 2–6 word visual search query?
- Does it describe physical objects or visible actions?
- Is it free of "Viewer...", free of sentences, and free of abstract concepts?

For all other compositions:
- Is `asset_query` strictly null?

### 9. Variant integrity

Is the variant actually allowed by the selected composition?

### 10. Presentation quality

Do labels and notes make the information clearer without changing its meaning?

### 11. Architectural boundary

Did you avoid legacy component selection and Remotion implementation?

### 12. Fallback honesty

If the composition cannot be populated truthfully, did you return `no_suitable_composition` rather than fabricate data?

FINAL STANDARD
==================================================

The goal is not:

"Choose something visual."

The goal is:

"Compile the semantic visual intent into the single registered composition that most faithfully communicates what the viewer needs to understand."

The ideal result has this property:

Visual Intent
→ preserved meaning
→ correct registered composition
→ accurate composition data
→ polished presentation
→ deterministic factual merge
→ safe validation
→ clean Remotion render

Never sacrifice semantic truth for visual variety.

Never sacrifice architectural correctness for creativity.

The best Composition Planner output is the one that makes the downstream renderer's job simple, deterministic, and faithful to the narration.
