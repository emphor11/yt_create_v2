HOW TO USE THE NARRATIVE PLAN
==================================================

The Narrative Plan is authoritative.

==================================================
CENTRAL TENSION
==================================================

Use the Narrative Plan's `central_tension` as the invisible thread connecting the body.

The script should progressively explain the tension rather than repeatedly stating it.

The viewer should feel that each scene is helping resolve the same underlying question or contradiction.

Do not restate the central tension at the beginning of every scene.

==================================================
STARTING BELIEF
==================================================

Use the Narrative Plan's `starting_belief` to understand the viewer's initial mental model.

Do not invent additional audience beliefs.

Do not repeatedly tell the viewer that they are wrong.

Instead, allow the explanation to naturally reveal why the intuitive interpretation is incomplete, inaccurate, or missing an important variable.

==================================================
ENDING UNDERSTANDING
==================================================

Use the Narrative Plan's `ending_understanding` as the destination of the body.

Do not reveal the final insight too early.

Build toward it.

The final scene should make the earlier scenes feel necessary.

The viewer should finish with the intended changed understanding rather than merely hearing the thesis again.

==================================================
SCENE ROLE
==================================================

Each scene has an approved `scene_role`.

Use it to determine HOW the narration should function.

Examples include:

- setup
- problem
- contradiction
- misconception
- context
- mechanism
- evidence
- example
- comparison
- consequence
- escalation
- reversal
- revelation
- implication
- synthesis
- resolution

Do not mechanically announce the role.

For example:

A `mechanism` scene should explain how something works.

An `evidence` scene should make the argument concrete.

A `revelation` scene should reveal something that changes the viewer's understanding.

A `resolution` scene should close the central question.

==================================================
VIEWER QUESTION
==================================================

Each scene contains a `viewer_question`.

Treat it as the cognitive question the viewer is carrying into or through that scene.

The narration should naturally answer, deepen, or advance that question.

Do not force the question into dialogue unless it sounds natural.

Do not begin every scene with a rhetorical question.

The viewer should feel the question through the logic of the narration.

==================================================
CORE TEACHING POINT
==================================================

`core_teaching_point` is the minimum learning outcome that the scene must deliver.

The narration must genuinely explain it.

Do not simply copy the sentence into the narration.

Expand it through reasoning, example, evidence, contrast, consequence, or explanation as appropriate.

By the end of the scene, the viewer should genuinely understand what the teaching point means.

==================================================
KEY EVIDENCE
==================================================

`key_evidence` is the approved factual foundation for the scene.

Prefer the scene's `key_evidence` when making concrete factual claims.

You may use the broader Research Context for additional context only when it is directly relevant and does not introduce a new argument.

Do not introduce facts merely because they exist somewhere in the ResearchPacket.

The scene's approved evidence should remain the primary factual anchor.

Never invent a new factual claim just to make the narration more interesting.

==================================================
NARRATIVE EXECUTION
==================================================

Every scene should perform ONE clear teaching movement.

A useful mental model is:

OPEN THE IDEA
→
EXPLAIN IT
→
MAKE IT CONCRETE
→
SHOW WHY IT MATTERS
→
CREATE THE NEED FOR WHAT COMES NEXT

Not every scene requires all five.

Choose what fits the scene role.

The important requirement is that understanding progresses.

==================================================
SCENE-TO-SCENE CONTINUITY
==================================================

The body must feel continuous.

The end of one scene should create the reason for the next.

Prefer:

established fact
→

→

→

→
mechanism
→

→

Do not write every scene as if the video is starting over.

==================================================
HOOK CONTINUITY
==================================================

The Hook has already happened.

The body must continue from it naturally.

Do NOT:

- repeat the hook's opening scenario
- repeat its exact wording
- restart the video
- introduce the same problem again
- restate the central curiosity from scratch
- write another introduction

Scene 01 should feel like the beginning of the answer to the tension created by the Hook.

The opening of Scene 01 should therefore feel like:

"Here is what is actually happening..."

not:

"Before we begin, let's understand..."

==================================================
PROGRESSIVE REVELATION
==================================================

Do not explain the entire argument in Scene 01.

Allow the viewer's understanding to deepen.

Reveal information in the order that makes the story most coherent.

Do NOT create artificial suspense.

Do NOT hide obvious facts merely to keep the viewer watching.

The goal is:

"Each answer reveals why another question matters."

==================================================
RETENTION THROUGH UNDERSTANDING
==================================================

Strong retention should come from progressive understanding.

Create forward momentum through:

- unanswered but meaningful questions
- contradictions
- hidden mechanisms
- consequences
- comparisons
- changing assumptions
- increasing significance
- surprising but supported implications

Do NOT use empty suspense.

Avoid:

"You won't believe..."
"This changes everything..."
"Wait until..."
"The shocking truth is..."

The explanation itself should create curiosity.

==================================================
ESCALATION
==================================================

Where the Narrative Plan supports it, make the significance increase.

For example:

"The mechanism exists."

→

"That mechanism affects ordinary decisions."

→

"The effect is larger than expected."

→

"That changes how the original problem should be understood."

Do not artificially dramatize a topic that does not require dramatic escalation.

The requirement is meaningful progression, not sensationalism.

==================================================
EXAMPLES
==================================================

Use examples when they materially improve understanding.

Distinguish:

- documented real-world examples
- illustrative examples

Never present an illustrative example as a real-world case.

When the ResearchPacket supplies an illustrative scenario, preserve that status.

Do not quietly turn its assumptions into established facts.

==================================================
ANALOGIES
==================================================

Use analogies when they genuinely make an abstract financial mechanism easier to understand.

A good analogy should:

- simplify the idea
- make it memorable
- preserve the important relationship
- help the viewer understand the real concept

Do not let an analogy replace the actual explanation.

Do not introduce unsupported factual claims through an analogy.

==================================================
SPOKEN LANGUAGE
==================================================

Write for the ear.

The script should sound natural when spoken by a professional narrator.

Use:

- natural spoken English
- conversational rhythm
- clear sentence structure
- concrete wording
- varied sentence length
- precise financial language
- controlled pacing

Avoid:

- academic prose
- textbook language
- corporate language
- unnecessary jargon
- long abstract sentences
- repetitive sentence structures
- generic AI language
- excessive rhetorical questions
- unnecessary qualifiers

The viewer should feel that an experienced human is explaining the subject directly to them.

==================================================
SCENE OPENINGS
==================================================

Do not begin every scene with:

"Now..."
"Next..."
"So..."
"Another thing..."
"Let's understand..."
"Let's take a look..."
"Moving on..."

Enter the idea naturally.

Every scene should feel like the next thought in the same argument.

==================================================
TRANSITIONS
==================================================

Use implicit transitions wherever possible.

Prefer causal continuity.

Example:

Scene 1:
"The payment looks affordable."

Scene 2:
"But that changes once the interest is included."

The second scene naturally grows out of the first.

Avoid repeatedly announcing transitions.

==================================================
ANTI-REPETITION
==================================================

Do not repeat:

- the same claim
- the same statistic
- the same example
- the same analogy
- the same explanation
- the same conclusion
- the same rhetorical question
- the same wording

unless repetition is genuinely required for comprehension.

When a concept has already been established, build on it.

Do not restate it from scratch.

RESEARCH IS THE SOURCE OF TRUTH

Do not use external model knowledge to fill gaps.

- omit the claim.

Never guess.

NUMBERS
==================================================

Every numerical value must be traceable to the supplied research context or be a simple, transparent calculation directly derived from supplied values when that calculation is genuinely required.

But do not turn that into an unrelated financial projection.

A calculated value is not a sourced statistic.

- population
- measurement type

Do not imply that a sample statistic represents everyone.

Do not tell the individual viewer what they personally should buy or do.
