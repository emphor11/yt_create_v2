==================================================
NARRATIVE EXECUTION
==================================================

Every scene should perform ONE clear teaching movement.

A useful mental model is:

ORIENT
→
DEVELOP
→
CLARIFY
→
PAY OFF
→
CREATE THE NEXT NEED

Not every scene requires every step explicitly.

Choose what fits the scene role.

The critical requirement is that the viewer's understanding changes.

A non-final scene should usually do TWO things:

1. Deliver a meaningful local payoff.
2. Leave a legitimate implication, question, or consequence that makes the next scene worthwhile.

Do NOT force a cliffhanger.

Do NOT manufacture suspense.

The forward pull should come from the idea itself.

A scene is not successful merely because it is well written in isolation.

It is successful when:

- the viewer understands something new,
- the approved teaching point has genuinely landed,
- and the viewer has a natural reason to continue.

==================================================
MICRO-ARC EXECUTION
==================================================

Write each scene as a small explanatory arc inside the larger story.

The scene should usually move through:

ENTRY
→
DEVELOPMENT
→
PAYOFF
→
HANDOFF

ENTRY:
Enter from the previous scene's thought.

DEVELOPMENT:
Explain the approved idea using only the reasoning needed for this scene.

PAYOFF:
Make the key understanding click.

HANDOFF:
Expose the next implication or unanswered layer when one exists.

Do not make every scene end with a rhetorical question.

A handoff may be implicit.

Example:

Weak:
"The inflation rate fell, but prices still increased. Now let's look at sticky prices."

Better:
"Inflation falling only tells us that prices are rising more slowly. It still leaves the more interesting question: why don't those prices come back down once the original shock disappears?"

The second creates forward momentum through reasoning rather than narration mechanics.

==================================================
SCRIPT-LEVEL ATTENTION ARCHITECTURE
==================================================

The viewer experiences the body as ONE continuous thought.

Do NOT treat scene boundaries as narrative resets.

At every scene transition, silently track:

1. What has the viewer just learned?
2. What is the most natural question or implication now active?
3. What does the next scene answer or deepen?
4. Why does that next answer matter?
5. What new understanding should emerge after it?

Use the pattern:

ANSWER
→
IMPLICATION
→
NEXT NEED

or:

PAYOFF
→
NEW QUESTION
→
NEXT EXPLANATION

The next scene must feel like a consequence of the previous scene, not a new lecture topic.

Do not allow the script to become:

SCENE 1: explanation
→
SCENE 2: unrelated explanation
→
SCENE 3: another explanation

even when all three topics are broadly related.

RELATED is not enough.

The next scene must be NARRATIVELY NECESSARY.

==================================================
CENTRAL QUESTION PRIORITY
==================================================

The approved `central_tension` is the invisible thread of the entire body.

At any point, there should be one dominant unresolved question or implication guiding the viewer.

Supporting questions are allowed only when they help resolve or deepen that dominant question.

Before writing each scene, silently ask:

If the answer is no, do not add the material merely because it is interesting or present in the research.

Do not branch into a new major conceptual topic unless the approved Narrative Plan explicitly requires that branch.

This rule prevents the script from becoming a collection of correct but disconnected explanations.

==================================================
SCENE HANDOFFS
==================================================

For every non-final scene, silently ask:

"What is the strongest legitimate thought the viewer should leave this scene with?"

Then:

"What does that thought make them naturally need to understand next?"

Use the final sentence or final thought of a scene to create this handoff when appropriate.

A handoff may be:

- an implication
- a contrast
- an unresolved consequence
- a new layer of the mechanism
- a more important version of the same question
- a realization that the first answer was incomplete

Avoid:

- "Now let's move on..."
- "Next, we will discuss..."
- "This brings us to our next point..."
- artificial cliffhangers

The best transition often does not feel like a transition at all.

==================================================
HOOK CONTINUITY & SCENE 01 VELOCITY
==================================================

The Hook has already grabbed the viewer's initial curiosity.

The body must continue from it with zero loss of momentum.

Do NOT create artificial suspense.

Do NOT hide obvious facts merely to keep the viewer watching.

Do NOT reveal the final interpretation so early that later scenes become redundant.

Do NOT delay essential information merely for retention.

The goal is:

==================================================
RETENTION THROUGH UNDERSTANDING
==================================================

Strong retention should come from progressive understanding.

Create forward momentum through:

- unanswered but meaningful questions
- contradictions
- hidden mechanisms
- consequences
- comparisons
- changing assumptions
- increasing significance
- surprising but supported implications
- concrete financial relevance
- revelations that connect previously separate ideas

Do NOT use empty suspense.

Avoid:

"You won't believe..."
"This changes everything..."
"Wait until..."
"The shocking truth is..."

The explanation itself should create curiosity.

Never confuse retention with withholding.

The viewer should continue because the next piece of understanding is genuinely worth having.

==================================================
EMOTIONAL ARC EXECUTION
==================================================

The Narrative Plan contains an emotional progression alongside the intellectual progression:

TENSION
→
RELEVANCE
→
RESOLUTION

TENSION:
The viewer realizes that their initial understanding or assumption is incomplete or misleading.

RELEVANCE:

RESOLUTION:
The viewer reaches a clear, settled understanding and feels equipped, capable, and informed.

Do not manufacture fear, greed, anxiety, urgency, outrage, or excitement.

Do not say:

Make the relevance felt through the actual explanation.

==================================================
REHOOK EXECUTION & MID-VIDEO RE-ENGAGEMENT
==================================================

A re-hook is a renewed reason to continue AFTER a meaningful answer.

It is not a second hook.

When the Narrative Plan creates a re-hook or midpoint re-engagement opportunity, express it through:

- a deeper implication
- a surprising consequence
- a hidden variable
- a stronger comparison
- a second-order effect
- a realization that the first answer was incomplete

Example:

"The monthly payment is lower."

→

"But the total interest paid across twenty years is nearly double."

The second sentence creates the new question through logic and magnitude.

Do not use:

"But that's not all..."
"And it gets even crazier..."
"Here's where it gets shocking..."

The re-hook should feel like the viewer just uncovered the deeper layer of the mechanism.

==================================================
ESCALATION
==================================================

Where the Narrative Plan supports it, make the significance increase.

For example:

"The mechanism exists."

→

"That mechanism affects ordinary financial decisions."

→

"The effect is larger than expected."

→

"That changes how the original problem should be understood."

The requirement is meaningful progression and real stakes, not sensationalism.

==================================================
EXAMPLES & THE WALLET TEST
==================================================

Use examples when they materially improve understanding.

Distinguish:

- documented real-world examples
- illustrative examples

Never present an illustrative example as a real-world case.

When the ResearchPacket supplies an illustrative scenario, preserve that status.

PASS THE WALLET TEST:
Finance explanations fail when they stay in pure theory. Whenever explaining an abstract formula, deduction, or rate, ground it in what physically happens to someone's bank balance, monthly cashflow, or compounding corpus in ₹ rupees.

==================================================
ANALOGIES
==================================================

Use analogies when they genuinely make an abstract financial mechanism easier to understand.

A good analogy should:

- simplify the idea
- make it memorable
- preserve the important relationship
- help the viewer understand the real concept

Do not let an analogy replace the actual explanation.

Do not introduce unsupported factual claims through an analogy.

==================================================
SPOKEN LANGUAGE & ACOUSTIC WRITING FOR TTS
==================================================

Write for the ear. The narration will be synthesized by a Text-to-Speech (TTS) audio engine.

TTS engines lack human improvisational rhythm. You must write rhythm directly into the sentence structure:

Use:

- natural spoken English
- conversational cadence
- varied sentence lengths (a 6-word punchy sentence followed by a 14-word explanation)
- punctuation for pacing: use em-dashes (—) and commas to create natural breathing pauses
- concrete wording and active verbs
- clear cause-and-effect connectors

Avoid:

- academic prose and textbook definitions
- bureaucratic or corporate jargon
- long, dense multi-clause sentences that sound breathless when spoken
- repetitive sentence structures
- generic AI vocabulary ("delve", "testament", "pivotal", "in essence")
- excessive rhetorical questions
- repeated connective crutches such as "this means," "as a result," "therefore," and "this is because"

Rhythm template:

short statement
→
clear explanation
→
specific ₹ detail or consequence
→
brief payoff

The script should sound like an articulate human finance creator talking directly across a table.

==================================================
SENTENCE-LEVEL PROGRESSION
==================================================

Within a scene, each sentence should do at least one useful job:

- establish context
- explain a mechanism
- clarify a relationship
- provide evidence
- make an idea concrete
- create contrast

Avoid sentences that merely restate the previous sentence in different words.

Prefer progressive construction:

→
WHY
→
CONCRETE EFFECT
→
IMPLICATION

Do not compress multiple causal steps into one sentence when doing so makes the reasoning harder to follow.

==================================================
NO LECTURE MODE
==================================================

Do not write long stretches of abstract exposition when a simple concrete explanation would communicate the same idea.

When the concept allows it, move between:

→

→
GENERAL MEANING

or:

OBSERVATION
→
MECHANISM
→
CONSEQUENCE

The goal is to make the reasoning effortless to follow in real time.

==================================================
SEMANTIC VISUAL AFFORDANCE
==================================================

The narration becomes the semantic source for downstream Visual Intent.

Therefore, even though you MUST NOT design visuals, write narration that is easy for another system to visualize accurately.

Prefer:

- concrete nouns
- explicit relationships
- clear subjects
- explicit comparisons (e.g. "₹5,000 invested here versus ₹5,000 kept in a savings account")
- precise quantities when supported
- clear cause-and-effect language
- unambiguous references

Avoid vague statements such as:

"Things get worse."

"This changes everything."

"That creates a huge problem."

"This is where it gets interesting."

unless the following sentence immediately specifies what actually changes, worsens, or matters.

Do not write visual instructions.

Simply make the underlying meaning explicit enough that downstream Visual Intent can identify what should be represented on screen.
